================================================================================
  ACT 4: KL DIVERGENCE TAB (15 minutes)
  Tab: KL Divergence (fourth tab)
================================================================================


COMPONENTS — LEFT PANEL
-----------------------
  1. Reference distribution display (pi_ref)
     Bar chart: 10 blue bars, one per token ("the", "a", "is", "of", "in",
     "to", "and", "it", "for", "on"). Heights are fixed — the frozen SFT
     model's next-token probability distribution.

  2. Policy distribution (pi) — DRAGGABLE BARS
     Same 10 tokens, purple bars. Drag up/down to change heights. Auto-
     normalises after each drag (probabilities always sum to 1).
     THIS IS THE CORE INTERACTION — physically reshaping the distribution
     builds visceral intuition for what KL measures.

  3. Stat pills (KL value, beta*KL penalty, beta)
     Updated in real time as bars are dragged.

  4. Beta slider for KL penalty
     Range: 0.00 to 1.00. Default: 0.20.
     Shows how the same KL divergence translates to different penalties.

  5. "Reset to Reference" button — sets policy = reference, KL = 0.


COMPONENTS — RIGHT PANEL
-------------------------
  6. Colour legend — pi_ref reference (blue), pi policy (purple), KL
     contribution per token (yellow).

  7. "Overlay: Reference vs Policy Distributions" grouped bar chart (SVG)
     For each token: blue bar (reference) next to purple bar (policy), with
     numeric values above each. Side-by-side visual comparison.

  8. "Per-Token KL Contribution" bar chart (SVG)
     Shows pi(x) * log(pi(x)/pi_ref(x)) for each token as a yellow bar.
     Values labelled above. Decomposes total KL into per-token contributions.

  9. "How KL Divergence Works" phase bar (purple border)
     Static explanation: KL >= 0, equals 0 only when distributions identical,
     measures asymmetric distance.


TEACHING STEPS
--------------
1. Point to the reference distribution (component 1): "These blue bars
   represent the SFT model's probability distribution over the next token.
   This is the 'safe' starting point."

2. Point to the policy distribution (component 2): "These purple bars are the
   current policy. Right now they match the reference exactly, so KL = 0."
   Point to stat pills showing KL = 0.000.

3. Point to the overlay chart (component 7): "Blue and purple bars are
   identical right now."

4. DEMONSTRATION 1 — Mild shift:
   Drag the "the" bar up slightly (from ~0.18 to ~0.25).
   - "I just increased the probability of 'the'. Watch:"
   - Point to KL value: "KL went from 0 to about [value]."
   - Point to overlay chart: "Purple bar for 'the' is now taller."
   - Point to contribution chart (component 8): "'the' has the largest
   KL contribution because it changed the most."

5. DEMONSTRATION 2 — Extreme shift:
   Drag "the" bar way up (~0.45), making it dominate.
   - "The policy is almost deterministic — 'the' nearly half the time."
   - "KL has jumped to [value]. This would be a huge penalty."
   - "Nearly all the KL comes from 'the'."

6. Click "Reset to Reference". KL returns to 0.

7. DEMONSTRATION 3 — Uniform shift:
   Drag all bars to roughly equal heights (~0.10 each).
   - "Every token is equally likely now."
   - "KL = [value]. Even though no single token dominates, the overall
   distribution is very different from the reference."
   - This shows KL captures distributional difference, not just individual
   token changes.

8. Click "Reset to Reference".

9. DEMONSTRATION 4 — Beta scaling:
   Drag one bar slightly (small KL ~0.02). Then adjust beta slider:
   - beta=0.05: "Low beta, penalty is just [value]. Barely noticeable."
   - beta=0.50: "Same KL, but now costs [value]. Significant."
   - beta=1.00: "Extreme beta, this tiny shift costs [value] in penalty."
   "This is why beta matters — it amplifies or dampens the same divergence."

10. Read the phase bar (component 9) to formally define KL properties.
