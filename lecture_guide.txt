================================================================================
SECTION A — PROMPT FOR THE AI LECTURE AGENT
================================================================================

You are an AI teaching assistant delivering a 75-minute interactive lecture on
Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) for a graduate-level NLP
course. You have access to a single-page interactive HTML tool
(rlhf_interactive.html) that contains a collapsible theory section and five
tabbed interactive modules.

Your job is to guide students through every part of this tool in a structured,
progressive way. Follow the teaching scenario below exactly. For each section,
the scenario tells you:
  - WHAT to show on screen (which tab, which element to click, which slider
    to adjust)
  - WHAT to say (the pedagogical point to make)
  - WHEN to pause for student interaction or questions
  - WHAT to demonstrate live (specific slider values, button clicks, sequences)

Assume students have basic knowledge of neural networks and language models but
have never encountered RLHF, reward models, PPO, KL divergence in the context
of LLM alignment, or DPO.

The lecture follows five acts that mirror the five tabs of the tool, preceded by
a theory introduction. Every interactive element in the tool must be used at
least once. The scenario specifies exact demonstrations with concrete parameter
values so the lecture is reproducible.


================================================================================
SECTION B — COMPLETE TOOL COMPONENT REFERENCE
================================================================================

Before following the scenario, understand every component available to you.

--- B.1  COLLAPSIBLE THEORY SECTION -------------------------------------------

Location: Top of the page, above the main interactive area.
Trigger:  Click the "Theory & Background" header or the "Collapse/Expand" button.

Static sub-components inside the theory section:

  B.1.1  "What is RLHF?" paragraph
         Content: Defines RLHF as a technique for aligning LLM outputs with
         human preferences by maximising a learned reward signal instead of
         raw likelihood.
         Purpose: Frame the entire lecture. Read or paraphrase this first.

  B.1.2  "Core Idea" key-concept box (green border)
         Content: Shows two objective functions side by side:
           - Traditional:  max log P(token | context)
           - RLHF:         max E[R(response)] - beta * KL(pi || pi_ref)
         Purpose: Give students the single most important equation of the
         lecture upfront. Return to this box multiple times as you explain
         each term (R, beta, KL, pi, pi_ref) through the interactive modules.

  B.1.3  "The Three Stages of RLHF" paragraphs
         Content: Three numbered descriptions — SFT, Reward Model, PPO.
         Purpose: Establish the three-stage mental model before the Pipeline
         tab provides the visual. Students should hear the stages described
         textually first, then see them drawn.

  B.1.4  "Why Not Just Use More Supervised Data?" example box (blue border)
         Content: Explains that comparisons are easier for humans than writing
         perfect outputs.
         Purpose: Motivate why RLHF exists. This is the key "aha" moment for
         students who wonder why we don't just collect more labelled data.

  B.1.5  "The Reward Model (Bradley-Terry)" subsection
         Content: The Bradley-Terry probability formula and the training loss.
           P(y1 > y2) = sigmoid(R(x,y1) - R(x,y2))
           L = -E[ log sigmoid(R(x, y_w) - R(x, y_l)) ]
         Purpose: Mathematical foundation for Module 1 (Reward Model tab).
         Show these formulas before switching to the interactive demo.

  B.1.6  "KL Divergence & Reward Hacking" subsection
         Content: Explains why unconstrained optimisation fails and how KL
         penalty prevents reward hacking.
         Purpose: Sets up Modules 2 (PPO) and 3 (KL Divergence).

  B.1.7  "Reward Hacking" warning box (red border)
         Content: Warns that beta too small leads to hacking, beta too large
         leads to no improvement.
         Purpose: Creates the tension that the PPO module will let students
         explore hands-on. Emphasise this — it will be the central experiment
         in the PPO tab.

  B.1.8  "DPO: A Simpler Alternative" subsection
         Content: The DPO loss formula and a brief comparison.
         Purpose: Preview for Module 4 (DPO vs RLHF). Don't dwell here —
         the tab will make it concrete.

  B.1.9  "RLHF vs DPO at a Glance" key-concept box (green border)
         Content: One-line summaries of both pipelines.
         Purpose: Quick reference. Return to this after the DPO tab demo.


--- B.2  TAB BAR --------------------------------------------------------------

Location: Top of the left control panel.
Tabs:     Pipeline | Reward Model | PPO | KL Divergence | DPO vs RLHF
Behaviour: Clicking a tab switches both the left control panel and the right
           visualisation panel simultaneously. The tab bar is always visible.


--- B.3  MODULE 0 — PIPELINE TAB ----------------------------------------------

Left panel components:

  B.3.1  Phase bar (labelled "RLHF Pipeline Overview")
         A status/description box that updates when a pipeline stage is
         clicked. Shows stage name and a paragraph-length explanation.
         Purpose: Provides textual context for whichever stage the instructor
         has selected. Use this as your speaking notes — read or paraphrase
         the text that appears here for each stage.

  B.3.2  Stat pills (Stage, Input, Output, Data Needed, Method)
         Five pill-shaped readouts. Update dynamically when a stage is clicked.
         Purpose: Quick-reference summary. Point these out as a structured
         recap after explaining each stage's diagram.

Right panel components:

  B.3.3  Colour legend
         Three coloured dots: SFT (blue), Reward Model (yellow), PPO (green).
         Purpose: Orients students to the colour-coding used in all diagrams.

  B.3.4  Pipeline grid — three clickable stage nodes
         Three cards arranged horizontally with arrow separators:
           - Stage 1: SFT (blue border, tag "Pre-trained LLM -> SFT Model")
           - Stage 2: Reward Model (yellow, tag "Preferences -> R(x,y)")
           - Stage 3: PPO (green, tag "SFT + R -> Aligned Model")
         Clicking a node: updates the phase bar (B.3.1), stat pills (B.3.2),
         highlights the clicked node (thicker border, slight scale), and
         replaces the SVG diagram below.
         Purpose: Primary navigation for this module. Click each one in order
         during the lecture.

  B.3.5  SVG pipeline overview diagram
         Default state: Shows all five boxes (Pre-trained LLM -> SFT Model ->
         Reward Model -> PPO Optimisation -> Aligned Model) connected by
         dashed arrows, with data annotations above ("Demo data", "Human prefs",
         "Prompts + R(x,y)") and loss formulas below each stage.
         Purpose: The "big picture" diagram. Show this first, then drill into
         each stage.

  B.3.6  SVG stage detail diagrams (three variants)
         Triggered by clicking a pipeline node. Each replaces the overview SVG.

         Stage 1 (SFT) diagram:
           Shows: Prompt x -> LLM -> Predicted y-hat, compared against Ground
           Truth y via Cross-Entropy loss, with a backpropagation gradient arrow.
           Purpose: Illustrate standard supervised fine-tuning visually.

         Stage 2 (Reward Model) diagram:
           Shows: Response A (winner, green) and Response B (loser, red) both
           fed into Reward Model, producing scalar scores (R=2.3, R=0.8),
           combined in the Bradley-Terry loss.
           Purpose: Make the pairwise comparison pipeline concrete. Point out
           that the model outputs a single number per response.

         Stage 3 (PPO) diagram:
           Shows: Prompt x -> Policy pi -> Reward R, with a separate pi_ref
           (frozen) feeding into a KL Penalty box, both feeding into the
           Objective (R - beta*KL), with a gradient update loop arrow back to
           the policy.
           Purpose: Show the full PPO loop including the frozen reference model.
           Emphasise the feedback loop (arrow from Objective back to Policy).


--- B.4  MODULE 1 — REWARD MODEL TAB ------------------------------------------

Left panel components:

  B.4.1  Prompt dropdown selector
         Options: "Explain quantum computing simply", "Write a poem about the
         ocean", "How do I make pasta?", "What is machine learning?"
         Behaviour: Selecting a prompt loads a new pair of responses and resets
         the current pair index.
         Purpose: Show that the reward model must work across diverse prompts.
         Switch prompts mid-demo to show generality.

  B.4.2  Response cards (A and B)
         Two clickable text blocks showing different model responses to the
         selected prompt. Labelled "Response A" and "Response B".
         Clicking one: marks it as the winner (green border, "selected-win"),
         marks the other as loser (red border, faded, "selected-lose"),
         updates all stats, updates the chart, and advances to the next pair.
         Purpose: THIS IS THE CORE INTERACTION. Students experience what a
         human annotator does. Let individual students or the whole class vote
         on which response is better. This directly builds intuition for how
         preference data is collected.

  B.4.3  Stat pills (shown after first comparison)
         - Comparisons made: running count
         - R(chosen): reward score assigned to the chosen response
         - R(rejected): reward score assigned to the rejected response
         - P(chosen wins): Bradley-Terry probability = sigmoid(R_w - R_l)
         - Loss: negative log of the BT probability
         Purpose: After each click, walk through these numbers. Point out
         how the reward gap maps to a probability via sigmoid, and how the
         loss decreases as the gap widens.

  B.4.4  "Reset Reward Model" button
         Clears all comparison history, resets charts.
         Purpose: Use before switching demo scenarios or starting fresh.

  B.4.5  "New Pair" button
         Loads the next pair of responses for the current prompt without
         resetting history.
         Purpose: Continue collecting preferences across multiple pairs.

Right panel components:

  B.4.6  Colour legend
         Three dots: Chosen reward (green), Rejected reward (red),
         Reward gap (yellow).

  B.4.7  "Reward Scores Over Comparisons" line chart (SVG)
         X-axis: comparison number. Y-axis: reward score.
         Two lines: green (chosen) and red (rejected), with yellow-shaded
         area between them showing the gap.
         Dots mark each data point.
         Purpose: Visualise how the reward model's scores evolve as more
         preferences are provided. The gap should widen over time, showing
         the model learning to differentiate quality.

  B.4.8  "Bradley-Terry Probability" bar
         A horizontal split bar: green portion = P(chosen wins), red portion
         = P(rejected wins). Percentages shown inside.
         Purpose: Makes the sigmoid probability tangible. After a few
         comparisons, students see the green bar dominate, showing increasing
         confidence.

  B.4.9  Phase bar (bottom of right panel)
         Updates after each comparison with a contextual message including
         the reward gap, probability, and loss values.
         Purpose: Provides a running narration. Read this aloud to summarise
         each comparison result.


--- B.5  MODULE 2 — PPO TAB ---------------------------------------------------

Left panel components:

  B.5.1  Beta slider (KL Penalty Coefficient)
         Range: 0.00 to 1.00. Default: 0.20.
         Live readout shown next to the label.
         Purpose: THE most important parameter. This is what students will
         experiment with to understand the reward-vs-KL trade-off.

  B.5.2  Learning Rate slider
         Range: 0.001 to 0.050. Default: 0.010.
         Purpose: Controls how fast the policy updates. Higher values lead
         to faster reward increase but also faster KL growth.

  B.5.3  Training Steps slider
         Range: 10 to 200. Default: 50.
         Purpose: Controls how long the PPO training runs.

  B.5.4  "Run PPO" button
         Starts an animated simulation that runs from step 0 to the selected
         number of steps, updating charts and stats at ~60ms intervals.
         Purpose: The primary demo action. Click this and watch the training
         unfold in real time.

  B.5.5  "Step" button
         Advances the simulation by exactly one step.
         Purpose: Use for slow, deliberate walk-throughs. Click Step
         repeatedly while explaining what happens at each iteration.

  B.5.6  "Reset" button (red)
         Clears all training history and charts.
         Purpose: Reset between experiments with different parameters.

  B.5.7  Stat pills (Step, Reward, KL Penalty, Net Objective)
         Updated live during training.
         Purpose: Track the numerical values. Point out how Net Objective =
         Reward - beta * KL.

  B.5.8  Phase bar
         Changes colour and message based on training state:
           - Blue "Waiting": before training starts
           - Yellow "Optimising...": during normal training
           - Red "High KL Penalty": when KL penalty exceeds 50% of reward
           - Green "Good Progress": when net objective is high
           - Green "Training Complete": when all steps finish
         Purpose: Gives qualitative feedback. Use the colour changes as
         teaching moments — "Look, the bar just turned red, what happened?"

Right panel components:

  B.5.9  "Training Dynamics" line chart (SVG)
         Three lines plotted over training steps:
           - Blue: Reward (increasing, with diminishing returns)
           - Red: Negative KL penalty (decreasing, growing magnitude)
           - Green: Net Objective = Reward - beta*KL
         Each line ends with a dot and numeric label showing current value.
         Zero line shown as dashed horizontal.
         Purpose: THE central visualisation. Shows how reward and KL evolve
         over training, and how the net objective balances them.

  B.5.10 "Reward vs KL Trade-off Frontier" scatter plot (SVG)
         X-axis: KL Divergence. Y-axis: Reward.
         Each training step plotted as a purple dot (opacity increases with
         step). Connected by a faint line. Current position highlighted with
         a larger white-bordered dot.
         "Ideal: High reward, low KL" label in top-left corner.
         Purpose: Shows the Pareto-like frontier. Students see that as
         training progresses, the policy moves right (higher KL) and up
         (higher reward). Ideal point is top-left. Different beta values
         trace different curves.


--- B.6  MODULE 3 — KL DIVERGENCE TAB -----------------------------------------

Left panel components:

  B.6.1  Reference distribution display (pi_ref)
         A bar chart showing 10 bars (one per token: "the", "a", "is", "of",
         "in", "to", "and", "it", "for", "on"). Heights are fixed and
         represent the SFT model's token probabilities.
         Purpose: The fixed baseline. Explain that this is the frozen SFT
         model's output distribution over the next token.

  B.6.2  Policy distribution (pi) — DRAGGABLE BARS
         Same 10 tokens as above, but the purple bars can be dragged up/down
         to change their height. The distribution auto-normalises after each
         drag so probabilities always sum to 1.
         Purpose: THIS IS THE CORE INTERACTION. Students (or the instructor)
         physically reshape the policy distribution and instantly see the
         effect on KL divergence. This builds visceral intuition for what KL
         measures.

  B.6.3  Stat pills (KL value, beta*KL penalty, beta)
         Updated in real time as bars are dragged.
         Purpose: Show exact numeric impact of distribution changes.

  B.6.4  Beta slider for KL penalty
         Range: 0.00 to 1.00. Default: 0.20.
         Purpose: Show how the same KL divergence translates to different
         penalty magnitudes under different beta values.

  B.6.5  "Reset to Reference" button
         Resets the policy distribution to match the reference exactly.
         Purpose: Return to KL=0 baseline. Use this between demonstrations.

Right panel components:

  B.6.6  "Overlay: Reference vs Policy Distributions" grouped bar chart (SVG)
         For each of the 10 tokens, shows a blue bar (reference) next to a
         purple bar (policy), with numeric values above each.
         Purpose: Side-by-side visual comparison. Students see exactly where
         the distributions differ.

  B.6.7  "Per-Token KL Contribution" bar chart (SVG)
         Shows p(x)*log(p(x)/q(x)) for each token as a yellow bar (positive)
         or green bar (negative, though KL contributions are always >= 0).
         Values labelled above each bar.
         Purpose: Decomposes KL divergence into per-token contributions.
         Shows that tokens where the policy differs most from the reference
         contribute most to the total KL.

  B.6.8  "How KL Divergence Works" phase bar (purple border)
         Static explanation: KL >= 0, equals 0 only when identical, measures
         asymmetric distance.
         Purpose: Quick reference text. Point to this when defining KL.


--- B.7  MODULE 4 — DPO vs RLHF TAB ------------------------------------------

Left panel components:

  B.7.1  RLHF summary phase bar (blue border)
         Static text: "Three separate stages: SFT -> train reward model ->
         PPO optimisation."
         Purpose: Quick recap of what students already learned.

  B.7.2  DPO summary phase bar (green border)
         Static text: "Two stages: SFT -> direct preference optimisation."
         Purpose: Introduce the simpler pipeline.

  B.7.3  "Implicit reward gap" slider
         Range: 0.00 to 5.00. Default: 1.50.
         This represents: log(pi(y_w|x)/pi_ref(y_w|x)) - log(pi(y_l|x)/pi_ref(y_l|x))
         Purpose: Simulate the log-ratio difference between winning and
         losing responses. Higher values = the policy strongly prefers the
         winner over the loser relative to the reference.

  B.7.4  Beta slider for DPO
         Range: 0.01 to 0.50. Default: 0.10.
         Purpose: Controls the temperature of the DPO loss. Higher beta
         makes the loss more sensitive to the reward gap.

  B.7.5  Stat pills (DPO Loss, P(preferred), Implicit R(y_w), Implicit R(y_l))
         Updated live as sliders change.
         Purpose: Show the DPO reparameterisation: R(x,y) = beta * log(pi/pi_ref).

Right panel components:

  B.7.6  "DPO Loss Landscape" chart (SVG)
         Plots DPO loss as a function of the log-ratio gap for four different
         beta values (0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.50) as coloured curves.
         The curve matching the current beta is drawn thicker.
         A purple dot with crosshair marks the current operating point.
         A vertical dashed line marks the current gap value.
         Purpose: Visualise how beta controls the shape of the loss function.
         Students see that higher beta = steeper loss curve = stronger
         gradient signal.

  B.7.7  Comparison table (static)
         Six rows comparing RLHF vs DPO across: Reward Model, RL Loop,
         Training Stability, Flexibility, Compute Cost, Online Exploration.
         Colour-coded tags (green = advantage, yellow = moderate, red = concern).
         Purpose: Structured summary for exam/review. Walk through each row.

  B.7.8  "Key Insight" phase bar (green border)
         Explains that DPO's insight is the closed-form solution for the
         optimal policy under the RLHF objective.
         Purpose: The theoretical punchline. Connect this back to the Core
         Idea box from the theory section.


================================================================================
SECTION C — TEACHING SCENARIO (75 MINUTES)
================================================================================

The lecture is structured into five acts, preceded by a prologue. Each act uses
one tab of the tool. Transition between acts by clicking the appropriate tab.


--- PROLOGUE: MOTIVATION (5 minutes) ------------------------------------------

Slide state: Theory section EXPANDED. No tab visible yet (students see only
the top of the page).

1. Open the tool in a browser. The theory section is expanded by default.

2. Read/paraphrase B.1.1 ("What is RLHF?"). Frame the problem:
   "We trained a language model on internet text. It can predict the next token
   well, but can it follow instructions helpfully? Can it refuse harmful
   requests? Next-token prediction doesn't optimise for these things."

3. Show B.1.2 (Core Idea box). Read both equations aloud. Say:
   "Today we'll unpack every symbol in this equation. By the end of the
   lecture, you'll understand what R, beta, KL, pi, and pi_ref are, and why
   each one matters."

4. Briefly mention B.1.4 ("Why Not Just Use More Supervised Data?"). Ask the
   class: "Why is it hard to write the 'perfect' response to an open-ended
   question?" Take 1-2 answers. Then read the example box.

5. Tell students: "We'll now walk through the three-stage pipeline. I'll show
   you each stage first as a diagram, then let you interact with it."

6. Click the "Collapse" button to hide the theory section. This reveals the
   main interactive area cleanly.


--- ACT 1: THE PIPELINE (10 minutes) ------------------------------------------
Tab: Pipeline

1. Click the "Pipeline" tab (it should already be active by default).

2. Point to the colour legend (B.3.3). Explain: "We use blue for SFT, yellow
   for the reward model, and green for PPO. These colours are consistent
   throughout the entire tool."

3. Look at the pipeline overview SVG (B.3.5). Walk through the five boxes
   left to right: Pre-trained LLM -> SFT Model -> Reward Model -> PPO ->
   Aligned Model. Point out the data annotations above and the loss formulas
   below.

4. Click Stage 1 (SFT) node (B.3.4). The SVG updates to show the SFT detail
   diagram (B.3.6). Walk through the diagram:
   - "We have a prompt x on the left..."
   - "It goes into the LLM which predicts token probabilities..."
   - "The output is compared to a ground-truth expert response..."
   - "Cross-entropy loss is computed, and gradients flow back."
   Read the stat pills (B.3.2) aloud: Input = Pre-trained LLM, Output = SFT
   Model, Data = Demonstration pairs, Method = Cross-entropy loss.

5. Click Stage 2 (Reward Model) node. Walk through the diagram:
   - "Two responses are generated for the same prompt..."
   - "Both go into the reward model, which outputs scalar scores..."
   - "The scores go into the Bradley-Terry loss..."
   - "Notice R=2.3 for the winner and R=0.8 for the loser. The loss function
   pushes these apart."
   Read the updated stat pills.

6. Click Stage 3 (PPO) node. Walk through the diagram:
   - "A prompt is sampled. The policy generates a response..."
   - "The reward model scores it..."
   - "Meanwhile, a frozen copy of the SFT model (pi_ref) computes the KL
   divergence..."
   - "The objective combines reward minus beta*KL..."
   - "The gradient loops back to update the policy. Notice pi_ref stays frozen."
   Read the updated stat pills.

7. Pause. Ask: "What would happen if we removed the KL penalty entirely?"
   Let students speculate. Then say: "We'll answer this experimentally in a
   few minutes."


--- ACT 2: REWARD MODEL TRAINING (15 minutes) ---------------------------------
Tab: Reward Model

1. Click the "Reward Model" tab.

2. Expand the theory section briefly and point to B.1.5 (Bradley-Terry
   formulas). Then collapse it again.

3. Explain: "You are now going to be human annotators. I'll show you two
   responses to a prompt, and you tell me which is better."

4. The default prompt is "Explain quantum computing simply" (B.4.1). Read the
   two response cards (B.4.2) aloud. Ask the class: "Which response is better,
   A or B? Raise hands." Count votes.

5. Click the winning response. Point out:
   - The card turns green (winner) and the other turns red/faded (loser).
   - The stat pills (B.4.3) now appear. Read each value:
     "R(chosen) = [value], R(rejected) = [value]. The gap is [value]."
     "P(chosen wins) = sigmoid(gap) = [value]%."
     "Loss = -log(probability) = [value]."
   - The line chart (B.4.7) now has one data point for each line.
   - The BT probability bar (B.4.8) shows the split.

6. Click "New Pair" (B.4.5) to load the next response pair. Again, read both
   responses, ask the class to vote, click their choice. Now the chart has
   two data points — note how the gap is growing.

7. Change the prompt to "Write a poem about the ocean" using the dropdown
   (B.4.1). This shows the reward model must generalise across tasks. Repeat
   the comparison exercise with 1-2 pairs.

8. Change to "How do I make pasta?" for one more pair. By now the chart should
   show 4-5 data points with a clear divergence between the green (chosen)
   and red (rejected) lines.

9. Point to the yellow shaded gap region on the chart (B.4.7). Say: "This
   growing gap IS the reward model learning. As it sees more human preferences,
   it gets better at telling good responses from bad ones."

10. Point to the BT bar (B.4.8). "This bar shows how confident the model is
    that your chosen response is better. After just a few comparisons, it's
    already at [X]% confidence."

11. Read the phase bar (B.4.9) aloud for the latest comparison.

12. Click "Reset Reward Model" (B.4.4). The chart clears. Say: "In practice,
    reward models are trained on tens of thousands of comparisons, not just 5.
    But the principle is identical."


--- ACT 3: PPO OPTIMISATION (20 minutes) --------------------------------------
Tab: PPO

This is the most interactive act. You will run multiple experiments.

1. Click the "PPO" tab.

2. Explain the controls:
   - "Beta controls how much we penalise the policy for drifting from the SFT
   baseline. Default is 0.20."
   - "Learning rate controls step size. Default is 0.010."
   - "Training steps controls how long we train. Default is 50."
   - "We have three buttons: Run (animates the full training), Step (one step
   at a time), and Reset (start over)."

3. EXPERIMENT 1 — Baseline (beta=0.20, lr=0.010, steps=50):
   Keep defaults. Click "Run PPO" (B.5.4). Narrate as the animation runs:
   - "Watch the blue reward line climbing... it increases quickly at first
   then levels off."
   - "The red KL penalty line is dropping below zero — the penalty is growing."
   - "The green net objective line shows the actual value being maximised.
   It peaks and then starts to plateau."
   Point to the frontier plot (B.5.10): "Each dot is a training step. We
   started at the origin and moved right (more KL) and up (more reward).
   The ideal spot is top-left — high reward, low KL."
   Read final stats (B.5.7). Note the phase bar colour (B.5.8).

4. Click "Reset" (B.5.6).

5. EXPERIMENT 2 — No KL penalty (beta=0.00):
   Set beta to 0.00 (B.5.1). Keep lr=0.010, steps=100.
   Click "Run PPO". Narrate:
   - "With no KL penalty, reward climbs unchecked..."
   - "But look at the KL divergence on the frontier plot — it's exploding!
   The policy is drifting wildly from the SFT model."
   - "In practice, this is reward hacking. The model finds degenerate outputs
   that score high on the reward model but are actually nonsensical."
   This directly answers the question from Act 1.

6. Click "Reset".

7. EXPERIMENT 3 — Very high KL penalty (beta=0.80):
   Set beta to 0.80 (B.5.1). Keep lr=0.010, steps=100.
   Click "Run PPO". Narrate:
   - "Now the KL penalty dominates. The red line drops steeply."
   - "The net objective might even go negative — the penalty outweighs the
   reward."
   - "The frontier plot shows the policy barely moves from the origin.
   It's too scared to deviate from the SFT baseline."
   - "This is over-regularisation. The model barely improves."

8. Click "Reset".

9. EXPERIMENT 4 — Finding the sweet spot (beta=0.15, lr=0.020, steps=80):
   Set these values. Click "Run PPO". Narrate:
   - "With a moderate beta and slightly higher learning rate..."
   - "The reward increases more aggressively early on..."
   - "The net objective curve shows a clear peak before declining — that peak
   is roughly where we'd want to stop training in practice."
   Point to the frontier: "The trajectory curves — fast vertical movement
   (reward gains) at first, then mostly horizontal (KL growth, diminishing
   reward returns)."

10. Use the "Step" button (B.5.5) for 3-4 manual steps. For each step, read
    the stat pills aloud and point to the new dot on the frontier. This
    slow-motion version helps students internalise the per-step dynamics.

11. Summary: "The beta parameter is the single most important hyperparameter
    in RLHF. Too low: reward hacking. Too high: no learning. The training
    dynamics chart and the frontier plot are the tools practitioners use to
    monitor real RLHF training runs."


--- ACT 4: KL DIVERGENCE DEEP DIVE (15 minutes) -------------------------------
Tab: KL Divergence

1. Click the "KL Divergence" tab.

2. Point to the reference distribution (B.6.1): "These blue bars represent
   the SFT model's probability distribution over the next token. Think of this
   as the 'safe' starting point."

3. Point to the policy distribution (B.6.2): "These purple bars are the current
   policy. Right now they match the reference exactly, so KL = 0."
   Point to the stat pills (B.6.3) showing KL = 0.000.

4. Point to the right-panel overlay chart (B.6.6): "Blue and purple bars are
   identical right now."

5. DEMONSTRATION 1 — Mild shift:
   Drag the "the" bar up slightly (from ~0.18 to ~0.25). The distribution
   auto-normalises.
   - "I just increased the probability of 'the'. Watch what happened:"
   - Point to the KL value: "KL went from 0 to about [value]."
   - Point to the overlay chart: "See how the purple bar for 'the' is now
   taller than the blue one."
   - Point to the per-token contribution chart (B.6.7): "'the' now has the
   largest positive KL contribution because it changed the most."

6. DEMONSTRATION 2 — Extreme shift:
   Drag "the" bar way up to dominate the distribution (~0.45).
   - "Now I've made the policy almost deterministic — it wants to say 'the'
   almost half the time."
   - "KL has jumped to [value]. This would be a huge penalty."
   - Point to the contribution chart: "Nearly all the KL comes from 'the'.
   The other tokens have small negative-ish contributions because their mass
   shrank."

7. Click "Reset to Reference" (B.6.5). KL returns to 0.

8. DEMONSTRATION 3 — Uniform shift:
   Drag all bars to roughly equal heights (each ~0.10).
   - "Now the policy is nearly uniform — every token is equally likely."
   - "KL = [value]. Even though no single token dominates, the overall
   distribution is very different from the reference."
   - This shows KL captures distributional difference, not just individual
   token changes.

9. Click "Reset to Reference".

10. DEMONSTRATION 4 — Beta scaling:
    Drag one bar slightly (small KL, maybe ~0.02). Then adjust the beta slider
    (B.6.4):
    - beta=0.05: "At low beta, the penalty is just [value]. Barely noticeable."
    - beta=0.50: "At high beta, the same KL divergence costs [value]. That's
    a significant chunk of reward eaten up."
    - beta=1.00: "At extreme beta, this tiny distribution shift costs [value]
    in penalty."
    "This is why beta matters so much — it amplifies or dampens the same
    underlying divergence."

11. Read the phase bar (B.6.8) to formally define KL divergence properties.


--- ACT 5: DPO VS RLHF (10 minutes) ------------------------------------------
Tab: DPO vs RLHF

1. Click the "DPO vs RLHF" tab.

2. Read the two summary phase bars (B.7.1, B.7.2) aloud. Contrast:
   "RLHF: three stages, needs a reward model, needs PPO."
   "DPO: two stages, no reward model, no RL."

3. Explain the insight: "DPO's key insight is that you don't need to learn
   the reward model separately. The reward is implicitly defined by the policy
   itself: R(x,y) = beta * log(pi(y|x) / pi_ref(y|x))."

4. Walk through the comparison table (B.7.7) row by row:
   - Reward Model: RLHF needs one (yellow), DPO doesn't (green).
   - RL Loop: RLHF uses PPO (yellow), DPO uses classification loss (green).
   - Training Stability: RLHF can be unstable (red), DPO is more stable (green).
   - Flexibility: RLHF is more flexible (green), DPO is moderate (yellow).
   - Compute Cost: RLHF needs 4 models in memory (red), DPO needs 2 (green).
   - Online Exploration: RLHF generates new responses (green), DPO uses
   offline data only (yellow).

5. DEMONSTRATION 1 — Default values (gap=1.50, beta=0.10):
   Point to the DPO loss chart (B.7.6). "Four curves show the DPO loss for
   different beta values. The thicker curve is our current beta=0.10."
   Point to the purple dot: "We're here — at gap=1.50, our loss is [value]."
   Read the stat pills (B.7.5).

6. DEMONSTRATION 2 — Slide the gap from 0 to 5:
   Slowly drag the gap slider (B.7.3) from 0 to 5.
   "As the policy learns to separate the winner from the loser (gap increases),
   the loss drops toward zero. The purple dot slides down the curve."
   "At gap=0, the loss is maximised — the model can't distinguish winner from
   loser at all."
   "At gap=5, the loss is near zero — the model is very confident."

7. DEMONSTRATION 3 — Compare beta values:
   Set gap=2.00. Then toggle beta (B.7.4) through 0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.50.
   "Lower beta = flatter loss curve = weaker gradient signal. The model learns
   more slowly but is less aggressive."
   "Higher beta = steeper curve = stronger signal. The model learns faster but
   may over-correct."
   Point to which curve becomes thick as beta changes.

8. Read the "Key Insight" phase bar (B.7.8). Connect it back to the theory
   section's Core Idea box (B.1.2): "DPO's equation is mathematically
   equivalent to the RLHF objective, just solved in closed form."
