TEACHING STEPS
--------------
1. Switch back to the Recursion Tree tab. Select Merge Sort as the final
   visual anchor.

2. Summarize the three methods:
   "Today we learned three ways to solve recurrence relations:
    - Recursion Trees: VISUALIZE the work at each level
    - Master Theorem: COMPUTE the answer from a, b, k in seconds
    - Substitution Method: PROVE the answer is correct with induction"

3. Emphasize when to use each:
   "Use the recursion tree for INTUITION — it shows you why an algorithm
   is fast or slow. Use the Master Theorem for QUICK ANSWERS on exams.
   Use substitution for FORMAL PROOFS when the theorem doesn't apply."

4. Highlight the key takeaway — memoization:
   "The most dramatic moment today was Fibonacci: from O(2^n) to O(n)
   just by storing results. That's dynamic programming. If you take one
   thing away from this lecture, it's that REMEMBERING prevents REDUNDANCY."

5. Connect to what's next:
   "Next, we'll study Dynamic Programming formally — building on exactly
   this idea of transforming exponential recursion into polynomial time
   by storing subproblem results."

6. End with the complexity comparison reference:
   "Remember our complexity table from the intro? The difference between
   O(log n) and O(2^n) at n=100 is the difference between 7 operations
   and a number with 30 digits. Solving recurrences tells you which side
   your algorithm is on."
